# Module 2: Modeling Data for Analysis

## Introduction

The databases that run a business are designed to record transactions quickly and accurately. The same design that makes them good at recording makes them hard to analyze. In this module, we learn **dimensional modeling**, the standard way to reshape data so that analysts and BI tools can answer questions quickly and consistently.

We start with the star schema, then move into advanced data management: tracking history, handling complex relationships, managing master data shared across the organization, comparing enterprise modeling approaches, and bringing it all together in a **data lakehouse**.

## Objectives

- Explain why the normalized designs used by transactional systems are difficult to use for analytics.
- Define facts, dimensions, and grain, and design a star schema for a business process.
- Choose a slowly changing dimension strategy to preserve history.
- Apply advanced patterns such as snapshot fact tables, conformed dimensions, and bridge tables.
- Explain how master and reference data management keep key business entities consistent.
- Compare enterprise modeling approaches, including Kimball, Inmon, and Data Vault.
- Describe how dimensional models are built and maintained in a data lakehouse.
